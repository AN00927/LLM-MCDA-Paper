### 1.3 The replacement mechanism, stated precisely

Write it as two separable facts, because only one of them supports the old
wording:

1. **On the marginal distribution, the compression is mild and does not point at
   0.5.** A_D's mean score runs 0.625 to 0.672 against the reference's 0.664.
   Its marginal sd runs 0.203 to 0.262 against 0.266, so the widest compression
   is GPT-OSS at 24 percent narrower. What is genuinely elevated is the share of
   scores inside [0.3, 0.7]: 0.431 to 0.550 against the reference's 0.368. So a
   weakened claim ("A_D puts more mass in the middle of the scale") survives;
   "collapses toward 0.5" does not, since the distribution is centred near 0.65.

2. **On the quantity that determines rank order, A_D separates more than the
   physics warrants, not less.** Every model's A_D within-scenario MAVT range
   exceeds the reference, by 1.25x (Qwen) to 2.00x (DeepSeek). On individual
   HVAC criteria the excess reaches 6.3x. A_D is not failing to discriminate. It
   is discriminating confidently in the wrong direction, which is why near-zero
   tau coexists with near-zero cross-run tau variance (the point line 1460
   already makes about internal consistency diverging from accuracy). That
   observation stands; only its stated cause changes.

Suggested one-line replacement for the mechanism, for B to adapt:
A_D's error is mis-ordering under confident separation, not indiscriminate
scoring: its within-scenario spread across alternatives runs 1.25 to 2.00 times
the physics reference's, while its rankings track the reference no better than
chance.

The A_E anchoring claim at line 1438 needs the same treatment. Exemplar scores
do pull the within-scenario range back toward the reference (DeepSeek 0.289 to
0.163; Qwen 0.181 to 0.173), so "exemplar scores anchor the output scale"
survives. "Counteracting the central-tendency bias that otherwise collapses
scores toward 0.5" does not.

## 2. A2: single-criterion top-1 recovery

Source: `paper/single_criterion_recovery.tex` (label
`tab:single-criterion-recovery`) and the `gt_top1_recovery_pct` rows of
`paper/dispersion_diagnostics.csv`.

How computed: share of scenarios in which the physics reference's top-ranked
alternative is also the argmax of one criterion taken alone. Ties break by first
occurrence, so it is deterministic. Three alternatives per scenario, so chance
is 33.3 percent.

**Test set, n = 195** (`source = single_criterion_recovery_test_195`). Use these
in the main text:

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

| Shower | 60 | 75.0 | 75.0 | 18.3 | 20.0 |

**Full corpus, n = 285** (`source = single_criterion_recovery_full_285`), already
carried in the table's tablenote:

| Decision type | Energy cost | Environmental | Comfort | Practicality |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| HVAC | 14.3 | 14.3 | 81.0 | 26.7 |
| Appliance | 68.0 | 55.0 | 93.0 | 92.0 |
| Shower | 73.8 | 73.8 | 20.0 | 21.2 |

What it supports: the benchmark is not degenerate. No single criterion recovers

point a reviewer asking "could one criterion do this?" needs answered.

Test and full-corpus figures agree in ordering everywhere and differ by at most
6.5 points (Appliance environmental, 46.2 against 55.0), so the Test-only
restriction does not change the argument.

## 4. A5.3: per-model p-values for the architecture comparisons

Source: `paper/per_model_pvalues.csv`, 24 rows, written by
`paper_pipeline/emit_per_model_pvalues.py`. Reads per-run xlsx already on disk;
no API calls.

**Why this exists.** `significance_testing.stouffer_combine` weights each model's
Z by sqrt(n) and combines as if the four tests were independent. They are not:
all four models score the same 195 Test scenarios against the same physics
ground truth, so their per-scenario differences are positively dependent and the
combined p is anti-conservative. `stouffer_combine` was deliberately left
unchanged (its own underflow-prone `1 - norm.cdf` at line 709 included). B should
replace the Stouffer-combined sentence with per-model reporting from the table
below rather than cite a combined p.

**How computed.** Wilcoxon signed-rank on the 195 paired per-scenario values,
one test per (adjacent architecture pair, metric, model). Z from the normal
approximation with the tie correction; two-sided p from `stats.norm.sf`. Two Holm
corrections are reported: `p_holm_subset` over the 24 tests in this file
(2 pairs x 3 headline metrics x 4 models), and `p_holm_family` over the full
56-test family that `significance_testing.run_wilcoxon` corrects across
(2 pairs x 7 metrics x 4 models). Quote the subset column unless B is also
reporting the other four metrics, in which case quote the family column. Both
give the same verdict everywhere.

### 4.3 Reading notes for B

- **All 24 tests are significant under both Holm corrections.** The per-model
  claim is therefore no weaker than the Stouffer claim it replaces, which is the
  useful outcome: dropping the independence assumption costs nothing.
- **n is 194, not 195, for GPT-OSS on the A_E-A_H comparison.** One scenario
  drops out of the paired set. Report it or use "n = 194-195".
- **Gemini's median paired difference is 0.0000 on A_D-vs-A_E tau and Top-1**
  while the test is still significant (p_holm 9.21e-03 both). The signed-rank
  test is driven by the distribution of nonzero differences, not the median. If
  B quotes medians as an effect size, Gemini needs a footnote or a different
  effect measure. Gemini is also the weakest of the four on this comparison
  (Z = 2.73 to 2.83 against 3.43 to 5.35 for the others).
- **Direction.** A_E beats A_D and A_H beats A_E on every metric and every
  model. Negative median diffs on tau and Top-1 and positive on MAE both mean
  the later architecture wins.

### 4.4 Did the A5.2 tie correction move these numbers?

Yes, slightly, and no verdict changed. I recomputed all 24 tests four ways
(tie-corrected or not, crossed with `norm.sf` or `1 - norm.cdf`). Results:

- **Largest shift in |Z| from the tie correction: 0.4283**, on
  `A_E vs A_H / Top-1 / gemini` (8.3196 to 8.7479). Its p moved from 8.83e-17 to
  2.17e-18, a factor of about 40.
- Typical shift is much smaller. On the MAE tests, where the metric is
  near-continuous and ties are rare, |Z| moves by 2e-06 to 9e-06. On tau and
  Top-1, where per-scenario values take few discrete levels, shifts run 0.001 to
  0.043 apart from the Gemini case above.
- The correction always **increases** |Z|, because dropping the tie term from
  the variance inflated it. The old code was conservative, so nothing that is
  significant now was non-significant before, and nothing lost significance.
- **Significance count is 24/24 both before and after.** No conclusion in the
  manuscript turns on this correction.
- **The `norm.sf` fix (A5.1) matters more for reporting than for inference.**
  Under the old `1 - norm.cdf` form, **15 of the 24 tests printed p = 0.0
  exactly**, an impossible value that a reviewer would flag. All 15 now carry
  real magnitudes, the smallest being 1.59e-32
  (`A_E vs A_H / MAE / gptoss`). If any manuscript sentence currently says
  "p < 0.001" or "p = 0.000" for these tests, replace it with the actual
  exponent from Section 4.2.

### 5.1 Filesystem mtimes are uninformative here

Every one of the 120 run artifacts carries an mtime between
`2026-07-29T20:41:22Z` and `2026-07-29T20:41:25Z`, a three-second window across
four models, three architectures and five runs each. That is the signature of a
bulk checkout or copy, not of the runs. Per (model, architecture) span is 0.0
hours in all twelve cells. **Do not report these as collection times.**

### 5.2 Git commit dates are the usable proxy

For each artifact the script also records the author date of the most recent
commit that touched it. Restricting to the `*_results_run_NN.xlsx` files, whose
commits track collection closely:

| Model | A_D | A_E | A_H |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| gemini | 2026-07-05 | 2026-07-21 | 2026-07-04 |
| gptoss | 2026-07-05 | 2026-07-21 | 2026-07-03 |
| qwen | 2026-07-05 | 2026-07-21 | 2026-07-03 |

**Overall window: 2026-06-28 to 2026-07-21, about 23 days.**

Structure worth noting: within each architecture the four models were collected
together (all four A_D arms in one commit day, all four A_E arms in a single
commit `8cae5266`), so **cross-model comparisons at fixed architecture are
contemporaneous.** Cross-architecture comparisons span up to three weeks. Since
every headline claim in the paper is an architecture comparison run within a
model, note the gap in the limitations rather than treating it as resolved.

### 5.3 Suggested limitation sentence

Adapt for B: benchmark runs were collected between 2026-06-28 and 2026-07-21.
Dates are recovered from version-control commit metadata rather than from
timestamps recorded inside the run artifacts, and bound collection from above.
Provider-side model updates within that window cannot be excluded.

This is the same gap CLAUDE.md flags under "Every run must record a UTC
collection timestamp". If B wants a stronger statement, the only route is new
data collection, which costs API calls.
